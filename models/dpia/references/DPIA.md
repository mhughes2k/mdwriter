# Step 1: Identify the need for a DPIA
-	Explain broadly what the project aims to achieve and what type of processing it involves i.e. how you will use personal data.
-	Summarise why you identified the need for a DPIA.  
You can refer or include links to other documents if helpful, e.g. project proposal/business case etc. 

# Step 2: Describe the processing
Describe the nature of the processing: 
-	How will you collect, use, store and delete data? 
-	What is the source of the data? 
-	How will you collect/access the data?
You might find it useful to refer to a flow diagram or other way of describing data flows. 

## Describe the purposes of the processing: 
-	What do you want to achieve? 
-	What is the intended effect on individuals? 
-	What are the benefits of the processing – for you, and more broadly?
-	Will the processing meet your intended purpose? 
-	Is there another way to achieve the same outcome? 


# #Describe the scope of the processing: 
-	Explicitly describe the data/type of data you will be collecting using.
-	Describe the category of individual it relates to, e.g. staff/students/research subjects.
-	Does it include special category or criminal conviction/offence data? 
-	How much data will you be collecting and using? How often? 
-	How many individuals are affected? 
-	What geographical area does it cover?


## Describe the context of the processing: 
-	How much control will individuals have over their data? 
-	Would they expect you to use their data in this way? 
-	Do they include children or other vulnerable groups? 
-	Are there prior concerns over this type of processing or security flaws? 
-	Is it novel in any way? 
-	What is the current state of technology in this area? 
-	Are there any current issues of public concern that you should factor in? 
-	Are you signed up to any approved code of conduct or certification scheme (if relevant)?



# Step 3: Consultation process
## Consider how to consult with relevant stakeholders: 
-	Describe when and how you will seek individuals’ views – or justify why it’s not appropriate to do so.
-	Who else do you need to involve within your organisation? 
-	Do you need to ask your processors to assist? 
-	Do you plan to consult information security experts, or any other experts?


# Step 4: Assess compliance against the Data Protection Principles
## i)	Lawfulness, fairness and transparency
-	What is your lawful basis for processing?  NB if you cannot identify this IGU can advise
-	How will you provide relevant privacy information, e.g. a privacy notice? 
-	Describe how your processing is fair and transparent. 


## ii)	Purpose Limitation
-	Are you using existing data for a new purpose? 
-	Will you use the data you collect in a way which is different to that initially described e.g. in privacy information/project plans etc?  If so, explain the different purposes here.  
-	If you are using data for a new purpose, is it compatible with the original purpose?


## iii)	Data minimisation
-	Are you collecting only the data that is adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary for your stated purpose? 
-	How will you review the collection/use/processing of personal data to determine whether it is still required?


## iv)	Accuracy
-	Describe how the accuracy of personal data will be monitored and maintained.


## v)	Storage limitation
-	How long will data be retained for?
-	Indicate the retention schedules/rule(s) which will govern how long this data is retained for.  Include any relevant links/appendices.
-	How will the retention rules be implemented, for records in any formats, e.g. hard copy or electronic?  Can any systems delete data according to the retention rules?


## vi)	Security, integrity and confidentiality
-	Who will have access to the personal data?
-	What technical and organisational controls will be in place to support the process and protect the personal data?

# Step 6: Identify and assess risks
## Describe source of risk and nature of potential impact on individuals. Include associated compliance and corporate risks as necessary.	
This is a multiple response question, formatted as a table with the headings: "Likelihood of harm", "Severity of harm", "Overall risk"

The "Likelihood of harm" aspect has the following options: "Remote", "Possible", Probable".

The "Severity of harm" aspect has the following options: "Minimal", "Significant", "Severe".

The "Overall Risk" aspect has the following options: "Low", "Medium", "High".

# Step 7: Identify measures to reduce risk
## Identify additional measures you could take to reduce or eliminate risks identified as medium or high risk in Step 6
This is a multiple response question, formatted as a table with the headings: Risk,	Options to reduce or eliminate risk,	

The "Effect on Risk" aspect has the following options: "Eliminated, Reduced, Accepted".

The "Residual Risk" aspect has the following options: "Low, Medium, High".

The "Measure Approved" aspect has the following options: "Yes/No".

The "Status" aspect has the following options: "Implemented, Pending, Planned, Other".
The "Other" aspect for status should offer a textual input.

# Final Step
After completing Step 7, you should submit your completed DPIA to the IGU for comment, along with any supporting documentation. The IGU will respond with comments/actions.
